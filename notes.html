2026-10-06

Why my STL weighs more than my CAD

The exported mesh of the L-bracket came out 0.7 mm³ heavier than the exact solid. With a smaller fillet it was only 0.1 mm³. Tessellation error is normal, but I expected it to make the mesh smaller, as it does for a convex part.

The sign comes from which side the material is on. A triangle edge spanning a curved surface is a chord, and a chord always lies inside the circle. On the inner fillet and in the screw holes, the centre of curvature is in the air, so the chords cut through air and the mesh claims a thin sliver of extra material. On a convex surface, a cylinder for example, the chords cut through material and the mesh comes out light.

Asegment = R²(θ − sin θ) / 2 ≈ R²θ³ / 12   per chord spanning angle θ

Open question: how the error should scale with R depends on whether the exporter limits the chord deviation or the angle. Going from R3 to R8 multiplied the error by seven, but the holes contribute too. Next step: export at a few fixed tolerances and fit it.

2026-10-06

An inside fillet adds material

Rounding an outside edge removes material. Rounding an inside corner does the opposite: it fills the square R × R corner and leaves out only a quarter disc.

ΔA = R² − πR²/4 = (1 − π/4)·R² ≈ 0.215·R²

So an inside fillet fills about 21 % of the square it rounds. On the bracket, R8 across a 30 mm width adds 412 mm³, 4.4 % of the part, all of it where the bending moment peaks. That's cheap stiffness, and it matched the CAD kernel to the second decimal.

2026-10-06

Counting holes with Euler

For any closed triangle mesh, vertices minus edges plus faces depends only on the number of through-holes g, the genus:

V − E + F = 2 − 2g

The bracket's STL gives −2, so g = 2: exactly two through-holes. A hole that stopped short of breaking through, a classic boolean-cut bug, would leave g = 1. It's one line in a test (mesh.euler_number in trimesh), and it checks the topology without looking at a single coordinate.
