About

Hi, I'm Alejandro.

A physicist moving into mechanical and product engineering, based in Bremen.

Physics first

I'm finishing a B.Sc. in Physics at Constructor University Bremen, with a minor in Global Economics and Management. Physics gave me the habit I rely on most: reason from first principles, put numbers on it, then check the numbers against reality.

My thesis is computational: Monte Carlo simulation and random-matrix analysis of the 2D Ising model in Python. In the lab I've operated a scanning electron microscope, characterised an electron beam with Helmholtz coils, and spent many hours on calibration, repeatability checks and uncertainty budgets.

Then hardware

At kiutra in Munich, which builds cryogenic systems, I worked first as an engineering intern and then as a working student. I laid out multi-layer PCBs in KiCad for measurement electronics that run below one kelvin, and assembled cryostat hardware: thermal buses, vacuum parts, low-noise wiring, measurement pucks. Then I tested what we built.

That's where it clicked. I like the moment a calculation meets a physical object and you find out whether you were right.

What I like working on

Mechanics, CAD, simulation, prototyping and testing: physical systems where the physics decides the design. I'm drawn to aerospace, energy, laser technology, mobility and sports engineering, and to work where a design has to survive a measurement.

I build parametric CAD in build123d and Onshape, and I prefer one clever simplification to three added features.

Off the bench

Over ten years of competitive kitesurfing and mountain biking. Both are physics problems you feel in your body: lift, drag, grip, suspension. Both have made me curious about the gear as much as the sport.

I speak Spanish natively, English fluently (C1+) and German at A2, improving in Bremen.
