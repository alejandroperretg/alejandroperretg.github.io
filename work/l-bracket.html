Why

Before trusting code-CAD with a real product, I wanted to run the whole workflow end to end on a part simple enough to check by hand: model, export, verify, draw. If the pipeline gets a bracket exactly right, I can trust it on parts where hand checks stop being possible.

The part

Two 40 mm legs, 30 mm wide, 4 mm wall. One Ø5.5 mm hole per leg, clearance for an M5 screw, 24 mm from the outer corner. An 8 mm fillet in the inner corner, where a bracket under load concentrates stress.

How it's built

Everything lives in one Python file. build123d unions two boxes, finds the inner corner edge by its position (the edge along Y at x = T, z = T), fillets it and cuts the two holes. If a parameter change ever makes that edge ambiguous, the script stops instead of rounding the wrong one.

The script exports STEP, the exact geometry for any CAD package, and STL, the triangle mesh for the slicer. A second script projects hidden-line drawing views from the solid and builds the project page from the same numbers.

Checking it

The volume can be computed three independent ways. If the model, the export or my maths were wrong, they would disagree.

V = 2·L·W·T − T²·W + (1 − π/4)·R²·W − 2·π(d/2)²·T
  = 9600 − 480 + 412.04 − 190.07 = 9341.96 mm³
MethodVolume, mm³Δ
Hand calculation9 341.96ref
CAD solid (OpenCascade)9 341.960.000 %
STL mesh, 1 164 triangles (trimesh)9 342.7+0.008 %

The mesh is watertight, and its Euler characteristic V − E + F = −2 gives genus 2: exactly two through-holes. Five pytest tests lock all of this in, so any future parameter change gets checked automatically.

What went wrong

  • The CAD kernel wouldn't load. Windows Smart App Control blocked OpenCascade's unsigned DLLs (An Application Control policy has blocked this file). Everything else installed fine, so the error only showed on the first real run. Fix: turn Smart App Control off, a one-way switch on Windows.
  • The first fillet was too timid. I started with R3 and raised it to R8. A bigger fillet eats into the flat seat the screw head needs, so I checked it: the fillet ends 12 mm from the corner, an M5 head starts at 24 − 4.25 = 19.75 mm. That leaves 7.75 mm to spare.
  • The mesh grew heavier than the solid. With R3 the STL differed from the CAD by 0.1 mm³; with R8 by 0.7 mm³. The flat triangles cut across the concave fillet on the air side, so the mesh holds slightly more material. A bigger curve means a bigger error. Note on this →

First prediction

Upright screwed to a wall, load hanging from the hole in the horizontal leg. Treated as a cantilever with lever arm L = 24 − 4 = 20 mm, ignoring the fillet, which makes it conservative:

σ = 6·F·L / (b·t²)   δ = 4·F·L³ / (E·b·t³)
PLA, σ ≈ 50 MPa (datasheet) → F ≈ 50 · 30 · 4² / (6 · 20) = 200 N at the root

That number is a prediction, not a result. Printed PLA is weaker across its layers, and the screws may let go first. The test will tell.

Next

  • Print it and weigh it against the predicted 11.6 g.
  • Stiffness test: clamp the upright, hang known weights on the hole, read the deflection with a dial gauge and fit E from the slope.
  • Load to failure in two print orientations to measure what the layer lines cost.
  • Add the fillet to the beam model and see how much of the gap it closes.
